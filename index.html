Baseline programming logic

This is the default architecture the AI coach should adapt around, not replace casually.

Hypertrophy: most upper-body work uses moderate-to-high weekly volume, stable exercises and double progression at RPE 8–9.
Weighted pull-up: two exposures/week — a heavier Tuesday session and lower-fatigue Saturday volume — with assistance/load progression determined by strict rep + RPE performance gates.
Skiing: Thursday develops lower-body force, unilateral control, eccentric hamstrings, isometrics and lateral power; Sunday progresses ski-specific work capacity before a Week 16 taper.
Recovery: pickleball counts as training load. Recovery cardio is easy RPE 3–4 and may be replaced by pickleball. The split deliberately separates the two upper/pull sessions from the main lower-body ski-strength day to limit overlapping local and systemic fatigue. Review fatigue about every four weeks; deload only when the data supports it.

Why 3 lifting days — not 4

This is a deliberate allocation decision, not a rule that four days are inferior.

The plan already supplies two upper-body/pull exposures and enough weekly upper-body work to support hypertrophy while reserving Thursday for ski strength. A fourth lifting day would mainly redistribute volume; it is not required to make the current dose effective. Because you also have Sunday ski conditioning, 2–3 pickleball sessions and easy aerobic work, the marginal benefit of another gym exposure is currently smaller than the value of preserving recovery. If session quality falls because Tuesday/Saturday become too long, or hypertrophy volume later needs to rise beyond what can be performed well in three sessions, a fourth day becomes a reasonable future option.

Nutrition direction

Direction: maintain to a very small surplus, not a conventional bulk or cut. Upper-body hypertrophy benefits from adequate energy availability, but every kilogram gained also increases the system mass you must move in a pull-up. Start around maintenance and allow only slow gain if training performance and measurements justify it. Aim for roughly 1.6–2.2 g protein/kg/day (about 120–165 g/day at 75 kg). Use bodyweight trend, gym performance and appetite/recovery to adjust rather than forcing scale gain.

Two-pass expert review trail

Pass 1 — specialist critique: hypertrophy review reduced unnecessarily expensive pull-up volume and favored stable accessory work; pull-up review replaced simple weekly load jumps with performance gates and later top-set/back-off work; ski review added explicit eccentric hamstring work, unilateral control, lateral power and a taper instead of endlessly adding circuit rounds.

Pass 2 — integration critique: the panel checked interference and recovery across all goals. The final architecture retained three lifting days, separated upper/pull stress from the main lower-body ski day, counted pickleball as real training load, converted calendar deloads to review points, and made progression performance-driven. Nutrition was also resolved as maintenance-to-small-surplus because aggressive mass gain conflicts with relative pull-up strength.

Evidence basis

Resistance training: the 2026 ACSM Position Stand synthesizes 137 systematic reviews and supports heavier loading for maximal strength, sufficient weekly volume for hypertrophy, full ROM and prioritising key lifts early in the session. PubMed PMID 41843416.
Effort regulation: hypertrophy generally improves as sets finish closer to failure, while failure itself is not mandatory; RPE is used here as the practical autoregulation language. PMID 36334240; PMID 38970765.
Concurrent load: aerobic + resistance training is broadly compatible, but lower-body strength can show some interference depending on modality/dose; this supports keeping recovery cardio easy and counting pickleball/ski work toward lower-body stress. PMID 37847373.
Ski preparation: alpine skiing places substantial eccentric/isometric demands on the legs; recreational skiing can leave eccentric quadriceps/hamstring fatigue for at least 24 h. This supports the eccentric, unilateral, lateral-power and taper elements. PMID 27900115; PMID 30622477.
Protein: a large meta-analysis found diminishing additional lean-mass benefit beyond about 1.6 g/kg/day on average, so the 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day range provides a practical buffer. PMID 28698222.

RPE guide

Use the rating for the hardest clean set, not how tired the whole workout felt.

RPE 6 ≈ 4 reps left · RPE 7 ≈ 3 left · RPE 8 ≈ 2 left · RPE 9 ≈ 1 left · RPE 10 = no additional clean rep.
Most compounds: RPE 7–9. Isolation hypertrophy: usually RPE 8–9, occasionally 9–9.5. Power/bounds: RPE 6–7 so speed and landing quality stay high.
